# MySMS Hardware & School Systems Integration Requirements

**Status:** Brainstorming draft for review; not approved for implementation  
**Prepared:** 10 November 2026  
**Scope:** Integration requirements and India-relevant device inventory. No code changes have been made.

## 1. Purpose

This document explores how MySMS could connect to equipment already present at Indian schools. It is intended to help decide which workflows and device families matter, what access vendors expose, which costs to budget for, and what MySMS should require before supporting a device.

The examples below are representative product families and integration references, not a claim that every school owns them or that every model supports an open API. Hardware, firmware, licenses, installer practices, local transport rules, and school network conditions differ. A model and firmware check with a real device is required before committing to support it.

### Initial product context

MySMS already has business domains that could own the records generated by devices:

- Attendance for student and staff attendance.
- SIS, people, HR, organisation, and academics for identities, class membership, employment, institution, and timetable data used to configure devices.
- Transport for routes, stops, and student transport assignments.
- Library for cards, catalogue circulation, and loans.
- Front office for visitor and reception workflows.
- Facilities for rooms and facilities.
- Finance for fee and canteen payments.
- Communications for parent/staff notifications after a valid business event.

MySMS uses separate domain modules, SDK contracts, and events rather than cross-module database writes. The repository review did not find a vendor-neutral hardware registry or device-ingestion contract. Any future integration should route observations through an owning module's API or event contract and must not write directly into another module's tables.

## 2. Working recommendations

1. **Connect to installed equipment before asking schools to replace it.** Discover the installed model, firmware, wiring, controller, vendor software, license, and available exports first.
2. **Keep a human fallback.** Manual attendance, a visitor book, a printed bus roster, or an import path must remain available during device, network, or power failures.
3. **Treat a device event as evidence, not automatically as a finalized business decision.** A gate scan may show that someone entered campus; it does not necessarily establish attendance in a particular class or lesson. A bus GPS coordinate does not prove a student boarded.
4. **Prefer vendor cloud APIs/webhooks where documented; use a school-side gateway for LAN-only equipment; keep controlled file import as a fallback.** These are complementary integration modes.
5. **Avoid collecting biometric templates and video centrally for routine integration.** Where biometric matching is already performed by a terminal, MySMS should receive the minimum identity reference and punch metadata needed for the approved workflow.
6. **Avoid a promise of universal device compatibility.** Maintain a tested compatibility list keyed by vendor, model, firmware, interface, and license terms.
7. **Start with event ingestion and status visibility.** Remote enrollment, changing credentials, opening doors, clearing logs, or other device commands should be separate capabilities requiring explicit authorization and careful audit.
8. **Design around intermittent power and internet.** Schools and buses may buffer events locally, deliver them late, or report inaccurate clocks. Duplicates and delayed delivery are normal cases the integration must handle.
9. **Treat child movement, vehicle location, plate numbers, and biometric identifiers as high-impact personal data by design.** Apply purpose limitation, access restrictions, short retention, and legal review before collecting or sharing them.
10. **Use the school's own source of truth for identity.** SIS/HR define students and staff. Device IDs, card numbers, vehicle plate numbers, or vendor user codes are external identifiers that need an explicit mapping.

## 3. Hardware and integration inventory

The cost notes are planning signals gathered on 10 November 2026. Marketplace prices can change, may be marked “available on request,” and may exclude installation, cabling, warranty extension, software, API access, or support. A school-specific quote is still required.

### 3.1 Staff and student attendance terminals

**Examples:** fingerprint/thumb terminals, face terminals, multi-modal face + fingerprint + RFID + PIN terminals, wall-mounted entry/exit devices, portable fingerprint scanners connected to a PC or tablet.

**Potential workflows:** staff in/out punches for HR/payroll; student arrival/departure evidence; campus entry events; optional canteen or library identity checks.

**Integration paths to investigate:**

- Vendor cloud/server push or webhook to a MySMS endpoint.
- Vendor REST or SOAP/ASMX API, SDK, or middleware server. Some eSSL documentation exposes an ASMX Web API, database/file export, and device commands; it is not safe to assume every feature is REST.
- Direct terminal TCP/IP or serial protocols only where the vendor documents and licenses them.
- CSV/Excel import, USB export, or a school-side connector for older/LAN-only models.

**India-relevant documentation:**

- [eSSL eTimeTrackLite Web Integration](https://esslsecurity.com/etimetracklite-web-integration) describes APIs for employee records, fingerprint/face enrollment, device commands, and transaction logs.
- [eSSL eBioserver New](https://esslsecurity.com/eSSL-eBioserver-New) describes device push, attendance-log queries, webhooks, and export options.
- [eSSL software and SDK downloads](https://esslsecurity.com/index.php/software) lists device communication, ePush, access-control, RFID, and other SDK resources.
- [Matrix COSEC Devices HTTPS Push API guide](https://matrixcomsec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/COSEC_DEVICES_PUSH_API_GUIDE.pdf) describes direct device communication with a third-party server for users, credentials, events, and commands.
- [ZKTeco India SDK portal](https://www.zkteco.in/SDK) and [ZKTeco F22 specifications](https://www.zkteco.in/fingerprint-access-control/F22) show that SDK/software and communication options are model-dependent.
- [Hikvision Time Attendance integration](https://tpp.hikvision.com/solutions/TimeAttendance-Integration) and [Hikvision OpenCapabilities](https://tpp.hikvision.com/tpp/OpenCapabilities) describe model/platform-specific integration options.

**Indicative cost evidence:** eSSL K30 Pro is listed at **₹6,019 including GST** and eSSL AiFace-Mars at **₹21,499 including GST** on Moglix; both listings say availability is on request. These are device-only examples, not installed or API-enabled totals: [K30 Pro listing](https://www.moglix.com/essl-identix-k30-pro-biometric-fingerprints-time-attendance-machine/mp/msn2vza0yvhx7l), [AiFace-Mars listing](https://www.moglix.com/essl-aiface-mars-attendance-biometric-machine-with-facial-recognition-face-card-password-verification-3000-face-template-capacity/mp/msnekpq3jxdw52). Vendor software/API access, setup, per-device licensing, local gateway, installation, UPS, and AMC may add costs. One India-based third-party biometric gateway currently publishes Native Push pricing of **USD $120–144 one-time activation plus $60–72/device/year**, optional protocol updates of **USD $120–300**, and Hybrid Push pricing of **USD $300–360 one-time connector plus $80–96/device/year**. Its self-hosted product is shown at USD $500–10,000 by scale. Custom integration charges may be separate. See [Cams Biometrics pricing](https://camsbiometrics.com/). Treat these as one vendor's published price ranges, not a MySMS fee or a universal device cost; confirm taxes, exact entitlement, data processing, and support in writing.

**Important workflow distinction:** a terminal punch should not silently complete a class/period attendance sheet. The school must decide whether a punch is staff timekeeping, student campus arrival, class attendance, or only an alert. A single student may generate multiple device events on one day.

### 3.2 RFID/NFC cards, key fobs, and readers

**Examples:** 125 kHz proximity IDs, 13.56 MHz contactless cards, MIFARE cards, NFC tags, handheld readers, wall readers, bus readers, canteen terminals, library self-check readers, and USB smart-card readers.

**Potential workflows:** school ID, staff clock-in, library issue/return, bus boarding/alighting, cafeteria/canteen purchases, door access, visitor badges, or parking permits.

**Integration questions:** Which card frequency and protocol is used? Is the system reading a card UID only, or performing secure card/application authentication? Does MySMS own the card number, or does an access vendor own credentials? Can one school card safely serve multiple uses? What happens on loss, replacement, transfer, graduation, or staff termination?

**Documentation:** [NXP MIFARE DESFire EV3](https://www.nxp.com/products/MF3DHx3) documents ISO/IEC 14443 Type A, AES options, and card security features. Device-to-controller reader links may use [OSDP](https://www.securityindustry.org/industry-standards/open-supervised-device-protocol/) with Secure Channel where supported, or legacy Wiegand/RS-485. These interfaces are not interchangeable; verify the reader and controller pair.

**Costs:** reader/controller plus card/fob issuance and replacement; secure-card personalization/key management; optional enrollment software; wiring/install/AMC. No single current India-wide price is reliable across frequencies and card security levels. Obtain quotes per card quantity, reader model, encoding, and installed door/bus/canteen count. Do not treat a publicly readable card UID as a secure credential.

### 3.3 QR and barcode scanners

**Examples:** USB handheld 1D/2D scanners, QR scanners at reception or gates, presentation scanners, mobile camera scanning, rugged handheld computers, and fixed-mount readers.

**Potential workflows:** student/staff ID validation, visitor pass check-in/out, library barcode circulation, bus boarding, inventory tagging, and fee receipt lookup.

**Integration:** many USB scanners emulate a keyboard (HID), while some provide a virtual COM/serial interface. The software must handle scanner focus, suffix characters, duplicate scans, kiosk mode, and phone-screen QR quality. Zebra documents both [USB HID and virtual COM setup](https://support.zebra.com/article/000019965?language=en_US) and scanner interfaces in its [DS2208 product reference guide](https://www.zebra.com/content/dam/support-dam/en/documentation/unrestricted/guide/product/ds2208-prg-en.pdf).

**Indicative cost evidence:** Moglix listed a Zebra DS2208 2D USB scanner for **₹5,999 including GST**, available on request: [listing](https://www.moglix.com/zebra-ds2208-2d-black-plastic-usb-barcode-scanner/mp/msnr50n4glv251). Budget extra for stands, cables, rugged/mobile models, spare scanners, kiosk/tablet hosts, and staff time to print/replace IDs. Basic USB HID may not require a separate API license; confirm for the chosen host and scanner.

### 3.4 Door access controllers, locks, turnstiles, and campus gates

**Examples:** RFID/fingerprint/face readers, access control panels, electric strike/magnetic locks, exit buttons, door contacts, speed gates, tripod/full-height turnstiles, flap barriers, and school-entry gates.

**Potential workflows:** time-bound staff/student/visitor credential access; door access events; anti-passback; alerting on forced door/tamper; and controlled access to labs, offices, hostels, or restricted areas.

**Documentation:** [Matrix COSEC device push](https://matrixcomsec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/COSEC_DEVICES_PUSH_API_GUIDE.pdf), [Hikvision device capabilities](https://tpp.hikvision.com/tpp/OpenCapabilities), [Hikvision ISAPI integration materials](https://tpp.hikvision.com/download/ISAPI_OTAP?type=1), and [OSDP](https://www.securityindustry.org/industry-standards/open-supervised-device-protocol/). Some vendor developer guides require agreement or account access.

**Costs:** hardware plus controller capacity, locks, power supplies, fire-release connections, civil work, cabling, reader enrollment, installation/commissioning, paid integration license, and AMC. Turnstiles and powered doors are site-engineered purchases; request an installed quotation. Exact equipment prices are not stable enough to quote without a site layout.

**Safety requirement:** MySMS should not operate door locks, turnstiles, or emergency egress/fire modes in the first integration scope. Initially consume status/events only. Any future control commands need a distinct approval, safety review, command authorization, failure-state policy, and audit trail.

### 3.5 Visitor reception and identity/ID printers

**Examples:** reception PC/tablet, QR invitation scanner, badge/photo printer, document/ID scanner, visitor kiosk, thermal receipt printer, and optional door intercom.

**Potential workflows:** pre-registered parent/contractor/visitor arrival, host approval, printed or QR visitor pass, visitor checkout, safeguarding watch-list alerts, contractor sign-in, and audit logs.

**Integration:** use a direct MySMS reception flow or connect to an installed visitor-management product via API/webhook/export. Avoid copying unnecessary identity-document data. If a pass grants door access, map it to a time-limited credential and revoke it at exit/expiry.

**Costs:** kiosk or tablet, scanner, badge printer, cards/labels/ribbons, kiosk management, installation, and any existing vendor license/API charge. Epson's [TM-T82X support page](https://www.epson.co.in/Support/Printers/Point-of-Sale/Receipt-Printers/Epson-TM-T82X/s/SPT_C31CH26461) is an example of manufacturer driver/software documentation; Moglix listed one TM-T82X-462 receipt printer at **₹15,159 including GST** when checked: [price listing](https://www.moglix.com/epson-tm-t82x-462-black-ethernet-pos-thermal-printer/mp/msnq94pyjqvek0). Quote per reception site and badge volume; this is not an installed price and receipt rolls are recurring consumables.

### 3.6 Parking, vehicle entry, and ANPR/LPR

**Examples:** number-plate recognition camera, barrier/boom gate, loop detector, RFID windshield tag, vehicle gate reader, intercom, visitor ticket printer, and access controller.

**Potential workflows:** staff/parent/visitor permit management, authorized-vehicle entry/exit logs, parking capacity, barrier status, and alerts. Keep plate recognition separate from student attendance unless a school explicitly approves a precisely defined workflow.

**Documentation:** [Hikvision OpenCapabilities](https://tpp.hikvision.com/tpp/OpenCapabilities) lists model/platform capabilities including ANPR and traffic events. Use a model-specific API/SDK and verify the camera/controller firmware and licensing. [ONVIF Profiles](https://www.onvif.org/profiles/) covers some IP video/access interoperability but does not guarantee consistent ANPR event fields across brands.

**Costs:** camera, lens/illumination, weatherproof housing, barrier/controller, safety loop/photocell, cabling, civil works, network, integration license, parking vendor subscription, and AMC. Quote required after lane/site survey. Plate data is personal data by design and should have a defined retention and access policy.

### 3.7 School-bus GPS, vehicle telematics, safety, and student boarding

**Examples:** GPS/GNSS tracker, AIS-140-compliant vehicle location tracking device (where applicable), cellular SIM/eSIM, panic/emergency button, driver identification terminal, bus camera/DVR, speed/ignition sensors, student RFID/QR reader, BLE beacon reader, and driver/conductor mobile app.

**Potential workflows:** live bus position during a trip, route deviation, arrival ETA, vehicle health, panic event, driver assignment, student boarded/alighted scan, parent notification, late/no-show reconciliation, and trip history.

**Integration options:**

- Consume the fleet provider's documented API/webhook. For example, [LocoNav developer documentation](https://developers.loconav.com/) describes fleet APIs; access, credentials, event coverage, quotas, and price need to be confirmed with the vendor.
- Connect to an installed vendor cloud rather than opening a connection to a bus modem from MySMS.
- If a school owns a documented tracker protocol, consider a separately isolated device protocol receiver. [Teltonika AVL protocols](https://wiki.teltonika-gps.com/view/Teltonika_AVL_Protocols) illustrate one vendor-specific binary protocol and TCP/UDP delivery; it is not a universal GPS standard.
- Student boarding evidence requires a separate RFID/QR/BLE/check-in source. A GPS tracker reports the vehicle's location; it does not identify the students aboard.

**India compliance reference:** [Parivahan's VLT & EAS portal](https://www.brvlts.parivahan.gov.in/home) describes AIS-140 VLT/Emergency Alert System processes for its state deployment. AIS-140 and school-bus requirements vary by vehicle/state and change; confirm with the relevant transport authority and ask suppliers for type-approval/fitment documents. [ARAI's AIS-140 approved-device list](https://www.araiindia.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2026/01/List-AIS-140-as-on-19.12.2025_Updated-1.pdf) is a supplier-validation reference, not a universal statement about a particular bus.

**Costs:** per-bus tracker and installation, approved fitment if required, SIM/data plan, vendor cloud subscription, API tier, GPS/event history retention, student reader and cards, camera/DVR/storage, panic button, and annual support. Current school-specific pricing is quote-only on providers such as [SkoolWay](https://www.skoolway.com/pricing/in/). Ask for a per-bus total-cost schedule over 3 years, including hardware ownership, SIM, cloud/app access, student RFID/NFC, API access, data retention, install, replacements, and cancellation/export.

### 3.8 Library, inventory, and document circulation equipment

**Examples:** 1D/2D barcode reader, RFID book labels, handheld RFID reader, self-service checkout kiosk, security gate/antenna, book label printer, receipt printer, and staff tablet.

**Potential workflows:** scan a student's library card and book barcode for issue/return; reservation pickup; inventory cycle count; gate alert; overdue notification. RFID may enable faster batch reads but requires a full reader/tag/gate ecosystem and a data migration plan.

**Documentation:** [Zebra scanner HID/COM setup](https://support.zebra.com/article/000019965?language=en_US), [NXP DESFire EV3 product reference](https://www.nxp.com/products/MF3DHx3), and [Zebra label-printer languages](https://support.zebra.com/article/Change-printer-language-and-driver-to-ZPL?redirect=false). A barcode scan is typically an input string; an RFID circulation system may expose proprietary software/API or file export.

**Costs:** readers, tags/labels, kiosk, gate, printer, ribbons/labels/receipt paper, device support, vendor software/API, and installation. Barcode starter setups are generally lower-cost than RFID gates/self-check systems, but exact prices depend on quantity and site layout; quote the complete system rather than comparing the unit tag price.

### 3.9 Fee counter, cafeteria, and point-of-sale equipment

**Examples:** Android POS terminal, card reader, QR stand/display, cash drawer, barcode reader, scale (where used), receipt printer, campus card reader, and payment soundbox.

**Potential workflows:** link a fee or canteen transaction to the MySMS student/guardian; accept payment; reconcile the provider's payment and settlement events; issue receipt; optionally debit a pre-paid campus card. Avoid storing payment-card credentials or PINs in MySMS.

**Integration:** prefer the payment provider's checkout, payment API, and signed server webhook over direct access to a POS terminal. [Razorpay webhook documentation](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/) describes asynchronous payment events. Existing MySMS finance records should be updated only after verified, idempotent payment confirmation; a client-side redirect is not authoritative proof of payment.

**Cost:** terminal lease/purchase or provider bundle, gateway platform fee/transaction charges, settlement and refund fees where applicable, SIM/network, receipt printer, paper rolls, POS support, and integration. As one current provider example, [Razorpay's pricing page](https://razorpay.com/pricing/) states a 2% platform fee plus applicable GST on standard gateway transactions; offers and merchant-specific pricing can differ. This is a transaction cost, not a device price or MySMS charge. Obtain the school's merchant-specific fee schedule.

### 3.10 CCTV, DVR/NVR, video doorbells, and security events

**Examples:** IP cameras, analog cameras with DVR, NVR, storage drives, PoE switches, video door phones, access cameras, and optional video analytics.

**Potential workflows:** device health, access-linked snapshot reference, security alert, ANPR event, or a staff-only link to the school's existing video system. Avoid moving full-time video feeds into MySMS without a separately approved product, bandwidth, retention, and access design.

**Documentation:** [ONVIF Profiles](https://www.onvif.org/profiles/) lists profiles for video and access-control product interoperability; conformance applies to specific products/profile combinations. [Hikvision OpenCapabilities](https://tpp.hikvision.com/tpp/OpenCapabilities) and [ISAPI/SDK download material](https://tpp.hikvision.com/download/ISAPI_OTAP?type=1) document vendor-specific options. Confirm the exact model, API, license, and event support before relying on it.

**Costs:** camera count, NVR/DVR, hard disks sized for retention, PoE/network switches, cabling, installation, UPS, VMS licenses, cloud storage/remote access, and AMC. Quote after a coverage and retention survey. ONVIF compatibility does not remove these costs or guarantee access to all analytics.

### 3.11 PA systems, bell controllers, intercoms, and classroom audio

**Examples:** IP speakers, analogue amplifier/zone speakers, bell scheduler, microphone, SIP/IP intercom, video door phone, paging controller, and emergency alert trigger.

**Potential workflows:** schedule a bell from the approved school calendar, page a class or campus zone, trigger a non-emergency audio reminder, or link an intercom ring to the reception screen. Emergency communication must stay operable if MySMS is unavailable.

**Documentation:** [Axis VAPIX Audio Systems](https://developer.axis.com/vapix/audio-systems/) and [Axis Intercom APIs](https://developer.axis.com/vapix/intercom/) expose APIs for those product families. Other installed systems may use SIP, proprietary controller APIs, serial, relay contact, or no integration interface.

**Costs:** zone speakers, amps, controller, IP intercom, wiring/installation, server/license, and AMC. Usually quote per building/zone. Any integration that broadcasts to classrooms needs a role-controlled preview/confirm path and a schedule/time-zone review.

### 3.12 Interactive panels, projectors, classroom devices, and device-management consoles

**Examples:** interactive flat panel, projector, document camera, teacher laptop, classroom tablet, casting adapter, charging cart, and vendor device-management platform.

**Potential workflows:** timetable-to-room display, scheduled device health, launch links, classroom inventory, and teacher-managed content/device status. Many panels are used through HDMI/USB-C or an app and may have no supported student-management API. Treat screen sharing as a classroom client capability, not as an assumption of hardware data access.

**Documentation:** for any chosen panel, verify its own management/API/MDM documentation. As an example of vendor material, [BenQ India education displays](https://www.benq.com/en-in/education/benq-board-interactive-displays.html) describes a hardware family; it does not establish one universal MySMS integration interface.

**Costs:** per-room panel/projector, mounting/cabling, installation, licenses for casting/management, extended warranty, replacement, and teacher training. Pricing is quotation- and model-dependent; include room count and lifecycle replacement in a school quote.

### 3.13 Facility, lab, utility, and environment sensors

**Examples:** temperature/humidity/air-quality sensors, water-tank/flow/leak sensor, energy meter, smart plug, generator/UPS status, HVAC controller, lab/freezer temperature logger, and building-management gateway.

**Potential workflows:** room/environment alert, maintenance ticket, energy/water dashboard, lab-temperature compliance, equipment fault, or classroom availability. Most schools may not expose these data, so this is an optional facilities phase rather than a baseline dependency.

**Integration:** devices may speak MQTT, Modbus, BACnet, or a proprietary cloud API; a local gateway is likely for fieldbus devices. Require units, calibration, sensor placement, measurement interval, alert threshold ownership, and safe control boundaries. Sources: [OASIS MQTT specifications](https://mqtt.org/mqtt-specification/), [Modbus specifications](https://www.modbus.org/modbus-specifications), and [ONVIF Profile M](https://www.onvif.org/profiles/profile-m/) for some video analytics metadata/events. No one standard covers every facility sensor.

**Costs:** sensors, field gateway, wiring, calibration, cloud usage, maintenance/replacement, and electrician/installer effort. Quote per sensor/site and required retention; low-cost devices may have proprietary cloud access fees.

### 3.14 Network, power, and edge-computing prerequisites

**Examples:** wired LAN, managed PoE switch, router/firewall, Wi-Fi access point, VLAN, mini-PC/industrial gateway, UPS, battery backup, mobile hotspot, static IP/domain, and time source/NTP.

**Why it matters:** a device can advertise TCP/IP yet be isolated behind a school firewall, attached to an undocumented vendor LAN, or have no reliable upstream internet. USB/serial hardware needs a nearby host, and gateway reliability depends on power and local storage.

**Costs:** survey, structured cabling, switch/PoE ports, firewall or egress rules, gateway hardware, UPS/battery replacement, ISP/static-IP, SIM/data, device enrollment, and onsite support. Price by campus/network scope, not per API connector alone.

### 3.15 Aadhaar registered biometric devices — separate regulated use case

Aadhaar authentication hardware/RD service is **not the default way to mark school attendance**. If the product ever needs Aadhaar authentication for a separately justified, legally reviewed use case, it must be designed as a separate integration. UIDAI says only Registered Devices are allowed in the Aadhaar authentication ecosystem, with the provider RD Service capturing, signing, and encrypting PID before it is passed to the application: [UIDAI Biometric Devices](https://uidai.gov.in/en/biometric-devices) and [Aadhaar Registered Devices Specification](https://uidai.gov.in/images/Aadhaar_Registered_Devices_2_0_Revision-7_of_Jan_2022.pdf). This can add a registered device, host OS/driver/RD Service, certified provider, application integration, and transaction/service charges. Obtain UIDAI ecosystem, provider, and legal guidance before considering it; do not use Aadhaar number as MySMS's student/staff identifier.

## 4. Proposed integration architecture requirements

These are product proposals for discussion, not existing MySMS features.

### 4.1 Connection modes

**Vendor cloud connector:** MySMS subscribes to vendor webhooks or queries a documented vendor API. Each connector owns that vendor's authentication, pagination, rate limits, retry policy, backfill, API-version compatibility, and vendor-specific field mapping.

**School edge gateway:** an optional service installed on a school-managed PC/mini-PC for LAN-only devices, push receivers, SDKs, serial/USB peripherals, or local exports. It should make outbound authenticated connections to MySMS, queue events durably when the internet is down, and resume without opening inbound internet access to the school LAN. It must support tenant-scoped identity, encrypted local storage, signed/controlled upgrades, health reporting, and documented uninstall/rotation steps.

**Controlled import:** for legacy/unsupported devices, a school administrator can upload CSV/XLSX or use an agreed export folder/SFTP connector. Show a preview, mapped columns, validation errors, duplicates, unknown people, date/time assumptions, and a commit summary before creating records.

### 4.2 Integration control plane

If approved, MySMS needs a deliberate place to manage external connections. A distinct integrations bounded context is a possible fit, but it is a product/architecture decision. At minimum, requirements include:

- Tenant- and institution-scoped vendor connections, sites, devices, capabilities, firmware, and ownership.
- Encrypted credential references, permissioned rotation/revocation, API entitlement and expiry tracking.
- External identifier maps for students, staff, visitors, vehicles, cards, devices, and vendor accounts.
- Sync cursors, import jobs, last successful sync, device/gateway health, queue depth, error counts, and retry state.
- Connector version and supported-model matrix.
- Audit trail for configuration, person mapping, credential changes, imports, and device commands.

Integrations must call the owning MySMS API/SDK or publish an accepted event for that domain. They must not write directly into domain database schemas. The MySMS event bus/outbox/inbox approach can support durable internal delivery after an external event is accepted.

### 4.3 Canonical observation/event envelope

Every accepted observation should preserve enough to explain where it came from, without retaining unnecessary sensitive payloads:

- Tenant, institution/site, source vendor/connection, device, and connector version.
- Source event ID or a deterministic idempotency key, plus source sequence/cursor when available.
- External person/card/vehicle reference, event type, and relevant business context.
- Device-reported time, offset/time zone if known, MySMS received time, and optional gateway time.
- Source payload hash and a short-lived redacted diagnostic payload only when needed.
- Processing result, duplicate/late/skew indicators, validation errors, and human resolution state.

The connector must make repeated delivery safe. It should detect duplicate IDs, multiple scans within a configured window, out-of-order/offline replay, device clock skew, invalid device identity, and events that cannot be mapped unambiguously. Do not silently infer identity from name, photo, or approximate match.

### 4.4 Identity and data ownership

- SIS/HR remain the source of truth for current people and institutional membership; attendance/transport/library/finance remain the source of truth for their respective business records.
- External device user IDs and card IDs are not global user IDs. Mappings must be unique within the vendor/device scope and retain valid-from/valid-to dates.
- Provide a review queue for unknown, duplicate, departed, or conflicting mappings. A person may have more than one credential and a credential may be revoked/reissued.
- Decide which MySMS changes are pushed out (new student/staff, class/route change, termination, card revoke) and which are pulled in. Do not enable two-way sync without field ownership and conflict rules.
- Never use a biometric template or face image as a cross-system identity key.

### 4.5 Offline and reconciliation behavior

- Devices and gateways may buffer events. Preserve original device time and receive time; use the institution's time zone for attendance rules, not the server host's zone.
- Queue events locally or at the vendor where supported, and track a cursor/high-water mark so reconnection does not drop or duplicate entries.
- Provide an outage dashboard, retry/backfill control, dead-letter view, and manual reconciliation path. A dead letter must identify the school, connection, date range, error, and recommended next action without exposing secrets.
- Define whether offline attendance is provisional or final. Conflicts with manual attendance must be visible and resolvable with reason, actor, and audit record.
- Do not delete source logs from devices as a normal sync action. Any destructive vendor command needs a separate administrator permission and explicit confirmation.

## 5. Functional requirements for a future approved implementation

### Device registration and configuration

- **HW-REG-01:** An authorized administrator can register a vendor connection and one or more sites/devices, recording make, model, serial/device ID, firmware, location, time zone, connection mode, supported capabilities, contract/license reference, and support contact.
- **HW-REG-02:** The admin can see connection health, last contact, last accepted event, last complete sync, gateway status, queue depth, and actionable error text.
- **HW-REG-03:** A device can be suspended, rotated to a new credential, or decommissioned without deleting its historical events.
- **HW-REG-04:** The system prevents cross-tenant or cross-institution event delivery and admin configuration.
- **HW-REG-05:** A connection can be tested with a harmless status request or sample event before attendance/finance/transport writes are enabled.

### Identity and roster synchronization

- **HW-ID-01:** The school can select which people groups are eligible for a device: staff, students, contractors, visitors, or another explicit class.
- **HW-ID-02:** External IDs are mapped to MySMS person/staff/student IDs with validity dates and source. Ambiguous or unknown mapping is quarantined for review.
- **HW-ID-03:** Roster and access updates can be previewed and reconciled. Deactivation, transfer, leave, graduation, and credential replacement behavior is defined by the school.
- **HW-ID-04:** The first release does not require importing raw fingerprint or face templates into MySMS.

### Attendance ingestion

- **HW-ATT-01:** The school configures whether an event represents staff in/out, student campus entry/exit, or another explicitly named attendance context.
- **HW-ATT-02:** Device events cannot complete or overwrite a class/period register unless the school has configured and approved that exact mapping.
- **HW-ATT-03:** Duplicate, late, conflicting, and offline replayed punches are idempotently handled and visible to an authorized reviewer.
- **HW-ATT-04:** Attendance provenance records source device/connection and lets staff distinguish device-created, manually entered, imported, and corrected marks.
- **HW-ATT-05:** Authorized staff can correct an erroneous device event with a reason; correction history remains auditable.
- **HW-ATT-06:** Staff and families have a non-biometric/manual alternative for failed or unavailable hardware, subject to school policy.

### Transport and student safety

- **HW-TRN-01:** GPS vehicle position is associated with an assigned vehicle and current route/trip, not inferred from a student's location.
- **HW-TRN-02:** Boarding/alighting status requires a student-specific scan, driver/conductor workflow, or another school-approved evidence source.
- **HW-TRN-03:** Vehicle and student events are kept distinct. Route deviation, no-scan, panic, and stale-location thresholds are configurable and do not generate duplicate guardian messages.
- **HW-TRN-04:** Parent visibility is limited to their own linked child and relevant active trip; school staff scope is role- and institution-based.
- **HW-TRN-05:** A bus provider can provide API limits, sample payloads, event IDs, history/backfill range, data location, SLA, hardware ownership, SIM terms, and cancellation/export terms before purchase.

### Visitor, access, parking, library, and payment workflows

- **HW-OPS-01:** Visitor check-in/out records have host, purpose, pass validity, location, and audit history; any legal-ID fields are optional and minimized.
- **HW-OPS-02:** Access/parking events are read-only by default. No remote unlock/open action is available until an explicit product and safety decision is approved.
- **HW-OPS-03:** A card/token can be revoked centrally and the system records whether revocation reached each enrolled device.
- **HW-LIB-01:** Scans are mapped to a specific student/staff library card and catalogue item; failed or duplicate circulation events are recoverable.
- **HW-PAY-01:** A payment becomes settled in MySMS only after a verified, idempotent provider event or reconciliation, not a browser redirect or device beep.
- **HW-PAY-02:** Card/POS terminals and MySMS never exchange or persist card PIN, CVV, or raw payment-card credentials.

### Imports and diagnostics

- **HW-IMP-01:** Imports support dry run, explicit date/time zone, column mapping, row-level validation, duplicate handling, retry, and a downloadable result.
- **HW-IMP-02:** A failed import can be retried without duplicating already accepted source events.
- **HW-OBS-01:** Diagnostics show useful device and event metadata while redacting passwords, tokens, biometric payloads, and sensitive personal details.
- **HW-OBS-02:** Support staff can view only the tenant/institution slice necessary for their authorized support task, with audited access.

## 6. Security, privacy, and safety requirements

- **HW-SEC-01:** All device and gateway traffic uses authenticated, encrypted transport. Where the device cannot do this, isolate it on a restricted LAN/VLAN and have the gateway terminate the vendor-specific protocol locally.
- **HW-SEC-02:** Per-connection credentials are secret-managed, never embedded in a mobile/web client, and support rotation/revocation without re-enrolling unrelated schools.
- **HW-SEC-03:** Webhook requests are authenticated and replay-protected; payloads are schema/size validated and rate limited.
- **HW-SEC-04:** Device and import actions are audited with actor, tenant, institution, source, timestamp, result, and reason where applicable.
- **HW-SEC-05:** Apply least privilege to device admin. Attendance readers should not be able to unlock doors, enroll fingerprints, export all people, or clear logs unless separately authorized.
- **HW-PRIV-01:** Default to keeping biometric templates on the device/vendor system. Do not build a central MySMS biometric repository as part of baseline attendance integration.
- **HW-PRIV-02:** Before a school enables student biometrics, face recognition, CCTV analytics, vehicle plate capture, or child-location sharing, record purpose, lawful basis, notices/consents, alternatives, retention, access roles, deletion process, and vendor/data-processor terms. Legal counsel should review the applicable DPDP Act/Rules commencement and school-specific obligations. Official references: [DPDP Act, 2023](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22037/2/a2023-22.pdf) and [MeitY DPDP Rules 2025 and enforcement timeline](https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa?pageTitle=Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Rules-2025%3B).
- **HW-PRIV-03:** For CCTV, prefer events/health or a link to the school's own NVR over copying video into MySMS. Document retention, camera coverage, access logs, and any restricted areas.
- **HW-PRIV-04:** Set separate retention periods for raw source payloads, attendance records, bus telemetry, visitor logs, plate events, and diagnostics. Avoid indefinite event retention merely because a device can store it.
- **HW-SAFE-01:** Hardware failure, internet loss, or MySMS maintenance must not disable emergency exits, fire release, panic buttons, or other life-safety systems.
- **HW-SAFE-02:** No face-recognition-based student surveillance or automated discipline decision is included in the baseline scope.

## 7. Proposed rollout order

### Phase 0 — school discovery and compatibility catalog

Collect a per-school inventory: vendor, exact model, serial, firmware, count, location, network path, vendor software, credentials owner, existing API/SDK/export license, current use, historical data access, install vendor, support contact, and renewal dates. Photograph labels and cables where permitted. Capture sample payloads/files with test data.

### Phase 1 — high-value, lower-risk imports

Start with a narrow staff attendance integration and controlled CSV/Excel import, then decide on student campus arrival separately. Prioritize event ingestion, status, identity mapping, duplicate detection, and manual reconciliation before remote credential enrollment or door-control actions.

### Phase 2 — transport tracking and student boarding

Select one GPS/fleet provider and one boarding evidence path (RFID/QR/mobile scan). Prove route assignment, stale GPS, offline device replay, out-of-order boarding scans, parent visibility, alerts, and data retention with a small fleet.

### Phase 3 — campus operations

Add library scanner/printer, visitor registration, selected access-control/ANPR events, or payment equipment based on actual customer demand and supported APIs. Keep commands and life-safety controls outside the first cut.

### Phase 4 — optional campus IoT and classroom systems

Only pursue smart panels, BMS, environmental sensors, PA, and utility telemetry where the school has an identified operational outcome, a supported protocol, and an owner for ongoing maintenance.

## 8. Procurement and vendor due-diligence checklist

Before a school buys hardware or MySMS promises an integration, request answers in writing:

- Exact hardware model, firmware, reader type, accessories, controller, current vendor software, and existing configuration.
- Supported API/SDK/protocol and versions; sample payloads; documented event IDs; command list; pagination; rate limits; backfill/history limits; and firmware upgrade policy.
- Does the device push events, require polling, store logs locally, support time sync, and recover events after internet/power loss?
- Can MySMS integrate directly, or is a separate middleware/server/cloud account required? Is API use included in the hardware price, annual software license, per-device license, or a paid partner agreement?
- Is there a sandbox/demo, test device, test tenant, test card, test user, and vendor engineer available for a proof of concept?
- What is the total 3-year cost: hardware, taxes, installation, cabling/civil work, enrollment, local gateway/UPS, cloud/license/API, SIM/data, transaction charges, AMC, replacement, support SLA, firmware updates, and decommissioning?
- Who owns the hardware, credentials, logs, and export when the school ends the subscription? Can it be moved to another system?
- What data is processed/stored in the cloud, where, for how long, and by which subprocessors? Can biometric templates, videos, IDs, and student location be kept local or disabled?
- What documented process handles an outage, lost card, failed fingerprint/face match, terminated staff member, class transfer, bus reassignment, and duplicate event?
- Can the vendor provide an updated ARAI/transport approval document if applicable, device safety certifications, warranty, installation sign-off, and an escalation contact?

### Cost components to budget separately

Do not compare only the device sticker price. A quote should separate:

- Device(s), controllers, readers, cards, cameras, gates, printers, accessories, spare units, and consumables.
- Installation, cabling, power, UPS, network upgrades, civil work, configuration, and data/roster migration.
- SDK/API/protocol license, third-party gateway, onboarding, custom integration, and annual support.
- Cloud subscription, per-device/per-bus/per-campus/per-user fees, storage/history limits, SMS/WhatsApp packs, and API tier.
- SIM/eSIM/connectivity, data usage, static IP/domain if required, and bank/payment transaction/settlement fees.
- AMC, response-time SLA, replacement stock, firmware/security support, warranty extension, and end-of-life migration.
- Applicable GST, currency conversion, and foreign transaction/bank charges for any USD-priced integration.

## 9. Open product and business questions

1. Which first customer segment matters most: schools with legacy biometric terminals, schools with fleet tracking, or greenfield schools?
2. Should MySMS integrate directly with device vendors or support a paid third-party biometric/device gateway where it reduces long-tail connector effort?
3. Which vendors/models should qualify for a supported list in the first version? eSSL, Matrix, and ZKTeco have visible integration material, but customer install-base evidence is still needed.
4. Is device software multi-tenant or school-local? Can MySMS operate a shared cloud connector safely, or does each school require its own server/gateway?
5. What exact meaning does the school want from “student attendance”: gate presence, full-day attendance, period/class register, bus presence, or several separate facts?
6. Should student attendance use biometrics at all? Which non-biometric options should be baseline (card, QR, teacher roster, or mobile check-in)?
7. Is student bus boarding included in transport subscription or an optional hardware/communications add-on? Who pays per bus and per student card?
8. Which device commands, if any, are required beyond read-only event ingestion? Should MySMS ever remotely enroll a credential or unlock a door?
9. What data retention and parent-facing alert policy is expected for attendance, GPS history, visitor logs, parking plates, and CCTV-linked events?
10. Are district/state transport mandates or board requirements part of the first launch markets? If yes, identify those jurisdictions for a specific compliance review.
11. Who installs, supports, and troubleshoots the on-prem gateway in a school: MySMS, school IT, device dealer, or integration partner?
12. What pilot scale and service levels should be assumed: students, staff, doors, buses, sites, daily peak events, internet uptime, and support hours?
